Map A: what is statistically associated?

No causal arrows

Study time is the original hypothesis. Past failures and family educational support are secondary comparisons. Solid lines mark statistically significant associations; a dashed line marks a non-significant result.

Map B: what might connect study time and grades?

Theory and scenarios · arrows are hypotheses, not estimated causal effects

Unobserved factors

Confounding and mediation

Prior mastery, motivation and family resources may relate to both study time and grades. Learning gained is a possible mediator. These mechanisms are not identified by the one-predictor model.

What the dataset cannot tell us

Motivation, sleep duration, study quality and learning gained are not directly recorded. Parent education and the yes/no family-support item capture only parts of a student’s context.

Map C: what helps predict held-out students?

Five-fold cross-validation on all 649 records · fixed folds, seed 42 · G1/G2 excluded

Predictive evidence

Random-forest reliance on recorded predictors

Importance is the mean drop in held-out R² after shuffling a variable’s encoded columns together. It is not a share of explained variance or a causal effect, and can exceed the model’s original R². Validation results remain the full-sample reference when Explore filters change.

Why a non-significant variable can still matter

Family educational support: the binary yes/no model has R² = 0.35% and p = 0.132 in the full sample. Its estimated difference is +0.39 grade points, with a 95% CI of −0.12 to +0.90. This does not establish that support has no effect.

Coarse measurement, sampling uncertainty and other student characteristics can hide relationships. A small R² is not evidence that confounding must be strong.

Policy implication: use past failures as a prompt for supportive follow-up, not a fixed label of ability. Do not allocate support only by study-time category or drop family context because one p-value exceeds 0.05.

Measure motivation, prior mastery and study quality before evaluating targeted support. Test proposed interventions with a design that can support causal conclusions.

04 · FROM FINDINGS TO A DECISION

A support conversation, then a small pilot.

Proposed · not tested
1 · INVITE

Listen privately.

A teacher invites students reporting difficulty to an optional, private conversation. Earlier failures can prompt an invitation, never an ability label or automatic placement.

2 · CHECK

Find the learning gap.

Use a short mastery check and ask about study methods, motivation and access to help. Confirm the student’s own priorities.

3 · AGREE

Choose one practical support.

Offer a two-week plan, such as targeted tutoring or retrieval practice. Extra hours are one possible choice, not the default prescription.

4 · REVIEW

Measure and adapt.

Compare baseline and follow-up mastery and student-reported burden. A consented comparison group would be needed to evaluate whether the support caused improvement.

Decision owner: teacher or counselor, with the student. Proposed evaluation: change in mastery, participation and burden; compare groups and report uncertainty. The old Portuguese sample does not validate this protocol or establish a policy for Korean schools.

06 · METHODS & LIMITATIONS

Make every conclusion traceable.

The investigation

We began by comparing study-time categories with final grades. Feedback led us to distinguish confounding, moderation and prediction, and to make the intended message explicit.

Because study time is recorded in four uneven ranges, we kept the actual categories. We did not split students into invented hourly values. We then compared a categorical model with numeric-code simple linear regression.

Our message is to interpret study time alongside a student’s history and context, while being clear about what the data cannot establish.

Data and coding

Original student-por.csv: 649 rows, 33 columns, two Portuguese secondary schools, final Portuguese grade G3 on a 0–20 scale. The original dataset accompanies a 2008 study; it is not a new Korean student survey.

  • Studytime 1: <2 hours/week; 2: 2–5; 3: 5–10; 4: >10.
  • All 15 recorded G3 = 0 grades are retained by default.
  • Past-failure raw codes 0–3 are preserved; categorical coding avoids assuming equal grade changes per code step.
  • G1/G2 earlier-period grades are excluded from predictor screening and the random forest.

The source documentation’s failure-coding note differs from the observed 0–3 values. We preserve the CSV values and disclose this ambiguity.

Testing and prediction

The primary categorical regression fits a separate mean for each study-time group. Its overall F test is equivalent to a one-way ANOVA under the same OLS assumptions.

The simple linear sensitivity model fits G3 = β₀ + β₁ × studytime code. In the full sample: G3 = 10.028 + 0.973 × code; R² = 6.24%; slope 95% CI = 0.682 to 1.264. The slope is per code step, not per extra hour.

Five-fold prediction checks keep each student out of their own training sample. Random forest: 150 trees, maximum depth 6, minimum leaf size 5, all 30 screened predictors.

Limits and next steps

These are observational, context-specific data. Self-reported study-time ranges are coarse; hidden variables and nonlinearity may affect the patterns. Ordinary CIs assume independent observations and the selected model’s error structure.

Filters describe subgroups; they do not establish causal control. Repeatedly choosing filters or predictors after viewing p-values increases false-positive risk. Positive-grade-only analysis changes the target population.

DDDM lesson: a small p-value and useful prediction answer different questions. Future work should collect recent local data, measure study quality and motivation, and preregister the intended comparisons.

Inspect full-sample sensitivity checks

The adjusted model includes past failures, school, sex, age, mother’s education, father’s education and travel time. Its partial F test is a conditional association, not proof that all confounding has been removed. HC3 is a heteroskedasticity-robust check.
